\section{Additional comparisons with denoising score matching}
\label{sec:supp_dsm_experiments}

We compare CINDES with denoising score matching (DSM) in three settings: a
bounded binary-tree density in dimension 31 and two unbounded compositional
densities in dimension 7. At each sample size
$n\in\{500,1000,1500,2000\}$, the methods use the same training data in each
of 20 paired replications. All tables report the mean $\pm$ sample standard
deviation across replications. The experiments assess different aspects of
generation: the binary tree has a log-density that factors into unary and
pairwise potentials, whereas the two compositional densities use a bounded
nonlinear tilt of a known reference density. The six coordinate pairs in the
compositional examples define the statistic used for evaluation; because of
the outer nonlinear transformation, they do not assert a Markov factorization.

\subsection{Bounded binary-tree density}
\label{sec:supp_bounded_tree}

\paragraph{Target distribution and data.}
Let $d=31$ and $\pi(i)=\lfloor i/2\rfloor$. On $[0,1]^d$, define
\begin{align}
 p_0(y)&=Z^{-1}\exp\{\ell_0(y)\},\qquad
 \ell_0(y)=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\phi_j(y_j)
             +\sum_{i=2}^{d}\psi_i(y_{\pi(i)},y_i),\label{eq:supp_tree_density}\\
 \phi_j(u)&=c_j\sin(2\pi u+\varphi_j),\qquad
 \psi_i(u,v)=a_i s_i\cos(2\pi(u-v))
              +b_i\sin(2\pi u)\sin(2\pi v).\nonumber
\end{align}
The coefficients are drawn once, using seed 11, with
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
a_i&\sim\operatorname{Unif}(0.385,0.715), &
b_i&\sim\operatorname{Unif}(0.22,0.44),\\
c_j&\sim\operatorname{Unif}(0.15,0.45), &
\varphi_j&\sim\operatorname{Unif}(0,2\pi).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
The signs $s_i\in\{-1,1\}$ are independent and equiprobable. All
coefficients are held fixed across replications. The tree has maximal
clique size two, although neither fitted method is given its edges.

Training and evaluation observations are sampled from a midpoint-grid
approximation $P_G$ to $p_0$, with $G=200$ intervals per coordinate. Each
cell receives mass proportional to the density at its midpoint. Tree
message passing supplies the root marginal and child conditionals for
sampling interval indices; coordinates are then sampled uniformly within
their selected intervals. An independent reference sample of 20,000
observations is shared across methods and replications.

\paragraph{Estimation and generation.}
Both methods use fully connected networks with three hidden layers of 128
ReLU units. CINDES fits a scalar density-ratio classifier against the uniform
reference distribution on $[0,1]^{31}$, with $2n$ reference observations and
classifier-logit bound $R=25$. Its diffused score is estimated using
$K=5000$ particles and support-adapted truncated-normal importance
sampling. DSM directly fits a time-conditioned, 31-output score network.
Both are trained with Adam (batch size 256, initial learning rate $10^{-3}$,
weight decay $10^{-5}$, and cosine learning-rate schedule). CINDES uses
approximately 20,000 optimization updates and DSM approximately 60,000.
Each fit generates 10,000 observations using the same reverse-time scheme
with $T=3$, $\delta=0.001$, and $M=200$ steps; final coordinates are
projected onto $[0,1]^{31}$.

\paragraph{Evaluation and results.}
For each coordinate, TV$_1$ is the histogram total variation discrepancy
between generated and reference samples, averaged over all 31 coordinates.
TV$_2$ averages the corresponding 20-by-20-bin discrepancy over the 30
parent--child pairs. Both use 20 equal-width bins per coordinate. These
statistics evaluate selected marginals, rather than full-joint total
variation. We also report the signed log-potential gap
\begin{equation}
 \mathrm{LLgap}
 =\frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{ref}}}\sum_{r=1}^{N_{\mathrm{ref}}}
   \ell_0(Y_r^{\mathrm{ref}})
  -\frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{gen}}}\sum_{r=1}^{N_{\mathrm{gen}}}
   \ell_0(\widetilde Y_r),
 \label{eq:supp_llgap}
\end{equation}
with $N_{\mathrm{ref}}=20{,}000$ and $N_{\mathrm{gen}}=10{,}000$ here.
The normalizing constant cancels; LLgap is a signed diagnostic of the mean
target log density, not a divergence. Table~\ref{tab:cindes_bounded_tree}
shows smaller mean TV$_1$, TV$_2$, and LLgap for CINDES at all four sample
sizes. At $n=2000$, the means are $0.0772$ versus $0.1325$ for TV$_1$,
$0.1954$ versus $0.2435$ for TV$_2$, and $3.8222$ versus $4.5416$ for
LLgap (CINDES versus DSM).

\subsection{Unbounded compositional density with sub-Gaussian tails}
\label{sec:supp_gaussian_composition}

\paragraph{Target distribution and data.}
Let $\phi_7$ denote the standard Gaussian density on $\mathbb R^7$, and set
\begin{align}
 p_g(x)&=Z_g^{-1}\phi_7(x)\exp\{1.5\tanh H_g(x)\},
 \label{eq:supp_gaussian_density}\\
 H_g(x)&=\frac{-u_4u_2-u_4u_5-u_2u_6-u_2u_3+u_5u_1+u_5u_7}
                  {v\sqrt 6},\qquad
 u_j=\tanh(x_j),\qquad
 v=\mathbb E_{Z\sim N(0,1)}[\tanh^2 Z].\nonumber
\end{align}
The bounded tilt gives this distribution full support on $\mathbb R^7$
and sub-Gaussian tails. Training observations are drawn by Gaussian-proposal
rejection sampling. The common, independent target-reference sample has
1,048,576 observations.

\paragraph{Estimation and generation.}
Both methods use two hidden layers of 64 Softplus units. Three-fold
cross-validation selects the $\ell_1$ penalty from
$\{0,0.01,0.1,0.3,1\}$ and a training duration capped at 2,000 epochs;
each selected model is refitted on all $n$ observations. CINDES uses the
Gaussian reference $q=\phi_7$, logit bound $R=25$, and $K=5000$
antithetic particles per score evaluation. DSM fits the residual score
$s_\theta(x,t)=-x+g_\theta(x,t)$. Neither estimator is supplied with
$H_g$ or its six generating pairs. Both generate 10,000 observations per
replication with $T=3$, $\delta=0.001$, and $M=200$ reverse steps, without
support clipping.

\paragraph{Evaluation and results.}
TV$_1$ averages coordinatewise histogram total variation discrepancies;
TV$_2$ averages them over the six pairs in $H_g$. Each coordinate uses 20
standard-Gaussian-quantile bins. The $H$-TV statistic compares generated
and reference histograms of $H_g(X)$ using 20 standard-Gaussian-quantile
bins, while $H$-$W_1$ is the one-dimensional Wasserstein-1 distance between
the unbinned empirical distributions of $H_g(X)$. LLgap is defined as in
\eqref{eq:supp_llgap}, with $\ell_0(x)=\log\phi_7(x)+1.5\tanh H_g(x)$.
Table~\ref{tab:cindes_accuracy_full} shows similar mean TV$_1$ values
and lower mean TV$_2$, $H$-TV, and $H$-$W_1$ values for CINDES at every
sample size. At $n=2000$, $H$-TV is $0.0633$ versus $0.0927$ and
$H$-$W_1$ is $0.0786$ versus $0.1294$ (CINDES versus DSM).

\subsection{Positive compositional density with exponential tails}
\label{sec:supp_exponential_composition}

\paragraph{Target distribution and data.}
On $(0,\infty)^7$, let
\begin{align}
 p_e(y)&=Z_e^{-1}q_Y(y)\exp\{1.5\tanh H_e(y)\},\qquad
 q_Y(y)=\exp\Bigl(-\sum_{j=1}^7 y_j\Bigr),
 \label{eq:supp_exponential_density}\\
 H_e(y)&=\frac{3}{\sqrt 6}
  \bigl(-u_4u_2-u_4u_5-u_2u_6-u_2u_3+u_5u_1+u_5u_7\bigr),
 \qquad u_j=1-2e^{-y_j}.\nonumber
\end{align}
Here $q_Y$ is the product of seven $\operatorname{Exp}(1)$ densities. The
bounded tilt introduces nonlinear dependence and preserves exponential
tails. Training and target-reference observations are drawn by proposing
from $q_Y$ and accepting with probability
$\exp\{1.5\tanh H_e(y)-1.5\}$. The common target-reference sample has
524,288 observations.

\paragraph{Estimation and generation.}
Both methods work in $z=\log y$, with known reference density
\begin{equation}
 q_Z(z)=\exp\left\{\sum_{j=1}^7(z_j-e^{z_j})\right\},
 \qquad z\in\mathbb R^7.
 \label{eq:supp_log_exponential_reference}
\end{equation}
They use networks with two hidden layers of 64 Softplus units. CINDES fits
the density ratio to $q_Z$ with logit
$f_\theta(z)=b-\operatorname{softplus}(a_\theta(z))$, where $b$ is learned.
DSM fits a time-dependent residual to the known diffused-reference score,
$s_\theta(z,t)=\nabla_z\log q_{Z,t}(z)+g_\theta(z,t)$. Neither method is
given $H_e$ or its generating pairs. For each method, three-fold
cross-validation selects the $\ell_1$ penalty from
$\{0,0.01,0.1,0.3,1,3\}$ and the training duration, capped at 2,000
epochs; the selected model is refitted on all observations. Both use Adam
with batch size 256 and initial learning rate $10^{-3}$.

The methods share an exponential-Euler reverse discretization with $T=3$,
$\delta=0.001$, and $M=400$ steps, initialized from the forward-diffused
reference at time $T$. CINDES uses $K=1000$ antithetic particles per score
evaluation, with a reference-score control variate. Each fitted model
generates 5,000 samples in log coordinates, which are transformed back by
$\widetilde Y=\exp(\widetilde Z)$.

\paragraph{Evaluation and results.}
TV$_1$ and TV$_2$ average histogram total variation discrepancies over the
seven coordinates and six generating pairs, using 20
$\operatorname{Exp}(1)$-quantile bins per coordinate, including an unbounded
final bin. We also compare the generated and reference distributions of
$H_e(Y)$ by $H$-TV, using 20 standard-Gaussian-quantile bins, and by the
unbinned empirical $H$-$W_1$. LLgap uses the signed reference-minus-generated
mean of $\ell_0(y)=-\sum_j y_j+1.5\tanh H_e(y)$. Table~\ref{tab:cindes_exponential_accuracy}
shows lower mean TV$_2$, $H$-TV, and $H$-$W_1$ for CINDES at all four sample
sizes. At $n=2000$, $H$-TV is $0.0573$ versus $0.0824$ and $H$-$W_1$
is $0.0676$ versus $0.1100$ (CINDES versus DSM). The mean TV$_1$
values are close, with DSM slightly lower at $n=2000$ ($0.0279$ versus
$0.0283$). The mean LLgap is closer to zero for CINDES at each size.
